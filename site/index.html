01 — The war

In 1945, about150 were left.

Germany invaded in 1939 and the breed registry died with it. Every square below is one surviving Polish Lowland Sheepdog. Two are marked — Kurta and Laska, the pair a vet found on a farm in the north.

02 — One sire

Ten litters.One dog.

Their son was called Smok — Dragon. He sired the first ten litters of the rebuilt breed and the standard was written from his body. Every PON alive today descends from him.

03 — The debt

One dog meansone gene pool.

Thin genes make sick dogs — allergies, autoimmune disease, cancer. UC Davis measured it: low diversity right across the genome, worst in the immune regions. They are not dying out today. The damage is in the DNA, not the headcount.

05 — Where it goes

A real charity isfixing the DNA.

The PON International Preservation Alliance maps PON DNA with the Orthopedic Foundation for Animals, so breeders can widen the gene pool instead of narrowing it. Registered 501(c)(3), EIN 39-3160948.
